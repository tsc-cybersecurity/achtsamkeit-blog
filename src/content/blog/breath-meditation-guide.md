Breath meditation (anapanasati) is perhaps the most universal meditation technique. It appears in Buddhist, Hindu, Taoist, and secular traditions alike. Its simplicity is its strength.

## The Technique

Find a comfortable seated position. You can sit on a chair, cushion, or bench — whatever allows you to be alert yet relaxed.

### Step by Step

1. **Settle in.** Close your eyes or soften your gaze. Take a few deep breaths to arrive.
2. **Find the breath.** Let your breathing return to its natural rhythm. Notice where you feel it most clearly — the nostrils, chest, or belly.
3. **Follow it.** Pay attention to the full cycle: the inhale, the brief pause, the exhale, and the space before the next breath.
4. **When the mind wanders** (and it will), simply notice where it went, and gently return to the breath. No frustration needed.
5. **Continue** for your chosen duration — start with 5 minutes and work up gradually.

## Common Challenges

**"I can't stop thinking."** You are not supposed to. Thoughts will arise. The practice is noticing them and returning to the breath, not eliminating them.

**"I feel restless."** This is normal, especially early on. Try counting breaths (1 to 10, then start over) to give the mind a gentle anchor.

**"I keep falling asleep."** Try sitting more upright, opening your eyes slightly, or practicing at a time when you are more alert.

## Deepening the Practice

As your concentration strengthens, you may notice subtler aspects of the breath — its temperature, texture, or the way it moves through the body. Let these observations arise naturally without forcing them.

With time, the breath becomes a doorway to deeper stillness and insight.
